### The First Lesson: Habakkuk 3:1–10, 16–18

**Reader:** A Reading from the Book of the prophet Habakkuk.

*In brief:* Habakkuk prays a prayer of awe, asking the Lord to renew his work in our own time and to remember mercy in wrath. He describes God coming in splendor, with light like the sun, the earth shaking and the mountains trembling at his approach. Then the prophet, trembling himself, resolves to wait quietly for the day of trouble. He ends in one of Scripture's great confessions of trust: though the fig tree does not blossom and the fields yield no food, he will rejoice in the Lord and exult in the God of his salvation.

*The full NRSV text is in your Bible or an authorized source:* [Habakkuk 3:1–10, 16–18 (NRSV)](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Habakkuk+3%3A1-10%2C16-18&version=nrsv)

**Reader:** The Word of the Lord.  
**People:** **Thanks be to God.**

### The Second Lesson: Luke 17:1–10

**Reader:** A Reading from the Gospel according to Luke.

*In brief:* Jesus warns his disciples against causing little ones to stumble, and tells them to rebuke a brother or sister who sins and to forgive when they repent, even seven times in a day. The apostles ask him to increase their faith, and he answers that faith even as small as a mustard seed can do astonishing things. He closes with the image of a servant who simply does what is commanded, so that we say: we are worthless servants; we have only done our duty.

*The full NRSV text is in your Bible or an authorized source:* [Luke 17:1–10 (NRSV)](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Luke+17%3A1-10&version=nrsv)

**Reader:** The Word of the Lord.  
**People:** **Thanks be to God.**

## The General Thanksgiving (BCP p. 101)

*Before the close of the Office one or both of the following may be used*

**Officiant and People:**

Almighty God, Father of all mercies,  
we your unworthy servants give you humble thanks  
for all your goodness and loving-kindness  
to us and to all whom you have made.  
We bless you for our creation, preservation,  
and all the blessings of this life;  
but above all for your immeasurable love  
in the redemption of the world by our Lord Jesus Christ;  
for the means of grace, and for the hope of glory.  
And, we pray, give us such an awareness of your mercies,  
that with truly thankful hearts we may show forth your praise,  
not only with our lips, but in our lives,  
by giving up our selves to your service,  
and by walking before you  
in holiness and righteousness all our days;  
through Jesus Christ our Lord,  
to whom, with you and the Holy Spirit,  
be honor and glory throughout all ages. Amen.
